Model reference

The PREVENT Equation

Every coefficient and transformation behind the American Heart Association's PREVENT risk calculator — the twelve base-model equations, written out so you can implement or audit them.

Source Khan et al., Circulation 2024;149:430–449 Models 3 outcomes × 2 horizons × 2 sexes Scope Base model (no UACR / HbA1c / SDI)

What it does

PREVENT (Predicting Risk of cardiovascular disease EVENTs) estimates absolute cardiovascular risk in adults aged 30–79 with no known CVD. It replaces the 2013 Pooled Cohort Equations with sex-specific, race-free models derived from over 6 million US adults, fitted on the age scale and adjusted for the competing risk of non-CVD death.

Two things make it structurally different from the PCEs. First, it predicts heart failure alongside atherosclerotic disease, so kidney function (eGFR) and BMI enter the model as predictors — the cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic framing. Second, it reports a 30-year horizon as well as 10-year, which is what makes it usable in people in their thirties and forties whose 10-year risk is near zero.

Race is not an input. Instead the model carries measurable biology (eGFR, BMI, and optionally HbA1c and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio) plus, in the full model, a ZIP-code social deprivation index.

Outcome 1 Total CVD ASCVD plus heart failure. Drives the ≥7.5% stage-1 hypertension threshold in the 2025 AHA/ACC BP guideline.
Outcome 2 ASCVD MI, stroke, or CV death. The risk figure used for statin decisions.
Outcome 3 Heart failure Incident HF. The only one of the three that uses BMI and ignores cholesterol.
Horizons

10-year risk is estimated for ages 30–79. 30-year risk is only defined for ages 30–59 — beyond that the equation is extrapolating past the observed follow-up and implementations refuse it.

How the risk is computed

Each of the twelve models is a single logistic-style equation: transform the inputs, take a weighted sum, squash it.

Center and scale each input

Cholesterol is converted mg/dL → mmol/L by ×0.02586. Blood pressure, eGFR and BMI each get a two-piece linear spline with a knot (110 mm Hg, 60 mL/min, 30 kg/m²), so the slope below the knot differs from the slope above it.

Reading the splines

Both eGFR terms are divided by −15, so the coefficients are positive: risk rises as filtration falls. Below the knot the slope is steep (β_eGFR⁻), above it shallow (β_eGFR⁺). The same trick makes SBP⁻ zero for anyone at or above 110 mm Hg, and BMI⁺ zero for anyone under 30.

Verification

These numbers were reconstructed, implemented, and then checked rather than transcribed.

The equation above was implemented from scratch and compared against the pyprevent reference implementation across 400 random patients spanning every valid input range, for all twelve models: maximum absolute difference 7×10⁻¹⁵ percentage points, i.e. floating-point noise.

It also reproduces the published worked example from the R package preventr exactly — a 50-year-old woman, SBP 160 on treatment, total cholesterol 200, HDL 45, diabetic, non-smoker, eGFR 90, BMI 35:

Total CVD14.7%53.0%

Before you build on it

Caveats
  • This is the base model only. The AHA calculator also offers "full" models adding urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, HbA1c, and a ZIP-code social deprivation index. Those have their own coefficient sets, not reproduced here.
  • Not a treatment-effect model. The AHA is explicit that PREVENT estimates risk at the current encounter; re-running it with a hypothetical lower SBP does not estimate the benefit of treating.
  • eGFR should be CKD-EPI 2021 (creatinine, race-free) to match how the models were developed.
  • Validate against the official calculator before any clinical deployment, and check for corrections — the original paper carried a published correction in March 2024.
  • External validations generally find good discrimination but some overestimation in certain health-system populations; PREVENT gives systematically lower ASCVD estimates than the Pooled Cohort Equations, which shifts who crosses statin thresholds.
